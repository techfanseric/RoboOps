## Design Context

### Users

RoboOps is used by robot companies, embodied AI companies, automation operators, platform delivery teams, and newly formed operating entities that are trying to turn deployed robots or automated equipment into a repeatable commercial business. Many users do not yet have a mature retail, support, finance, maintenance, or field-operations team.

### Brand Personality

Professional, operational, and grounded. The interface should feel like a serious business control room for daily operations: calm enough for repeated use, explicit enough to teach a new team how to operate, and trustworthy enough for approvals, refunds, incident handling, and audit trails.

### Aesthetic Direction

Restrained product UI with dense but readable information. Avoid marketing-style hero layouts, decorative dashboards, and demo-like copy. The first screen should always feel like the actual working console, not a landing page.

### Design Principles

- Treat every module as part of a business loop: configuration, release, operation, exception handling, task execution, approval, and audit.
- Use generic platform language such as point, robot/device, product/service, business request, incident, task, release, role, data scope, and approval. Beverage wording only belongs in scenario examples.
- Prefer concrete actions and state changes over explanatory placeholder text.
- High-risk actions must expose owner, reason, effect, approval state, and audit trail.
- Help customers build an operating team through configurable templates instead of hard-coded roles.
